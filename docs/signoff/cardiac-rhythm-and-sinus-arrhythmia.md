# Cardiac rhythm and sinus arrhythmia

## Why this matters

An irregular rhythm that would alarm a human clinician is normal in a resting dog, and a monitor read by human habit will misfire.

## Claims that differ from human practice

- Respiratory sinus arrhythmia is large and normal in a resting dog, more than three times the human amplitude in one study.
- It is less marked at higher heart rates and absent during panting.

## Dog vs human compare rows

- **Sinus arrhythmia at rest** (human basis: general-medical-knowledge): human: Adult sinus rhythm is regular, with small respiratory variation. | canine: Dogs have a clear respiratory sinus arrhythmia at rest. It averaged 40.1% of mean heart rate (peak to trough) in one study, more than three times the human value, and explained less than half of the canine sinus arrhythmia. Dog and human beat-to-beat patterns differ: the dog shows non-linear, wider interval distributions.
- **Effect of heart rate and panting** (human basis: general-medical-knowledge): human: Not applicable. | canine: Sinus arrhythmia was less marked at high heart rates in a large Beagle ECG study but did not disappear. In two dogs, respiratory sinus arrhythmia was absent during panting.
- **Loss of sinus arrhythmia** (human basis: general-medical-knowledge): human: Not applicable. | canine: In dogs with myxomatous mitral valve disease, loss of respiratory sinus arrhythmia is described as a negative prognostic factor, and the percentage decreased with disease severity up to acute congestive heart failure.
