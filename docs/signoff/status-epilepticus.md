## Why this matters

Seizure emergencies are unlikely to stop on their own and become harder to treat the longer they last, with high morbidity and mortality [1][5].

## Claims that differ from human practice

- Intranasal benzodiazepine by atomizer has been studied in dogs as a first-line option when IV access is not available [2][3].
- Treat early: prolonged seizure activity becomes progressively more resistant to treatment [5].

## Recognition

- Status epilepticus is prolonged epileptic seizure activity. Untreated, it can cause irreversible cerebral damage and systemic complications [4].

## Management

- Benzodiazepines are the first-line drugs for canine status epilepticus and cluster seizures [1][4].
- An ACVIM consensus statement recommends an early, rapid, stage-based approach, with management of complications and underlying causes alongside seizure medication [1].
- In a randomized trial in dogs in status epilepticus before IV access, intranasal midazolam by atomizer stopped seizures in 14 of 20 dogs versus 3 of 15 with rectal diazepam [2].
- Intranasal and intravenous midazolam had similar success (76% and 61%), and intranasal was faster when the time to place an IV catheter was counted [3].
- Sedation and ataxia were common after benzodiazepine treatment [2][3].
- In an owner survey, intranasal midazolam was perceived as more effective than rectal diazepam. This is owner perception, not a trial [5].
