# Cocaine

## Why this matters

Cocaine toxicosis is infrequently suspected in dogs, and neurological signs predominate with frequent cardiovascular effects [1].

## Claims that differ from human practice

- Neurological signs predominate, but cardiovascular signs are frequent, and seizures, hypertensive crisis and tachyarrhythmia are possible [1].

## Recognition

- In 19 dogs with a positive urine cocaine test, all had neurological abnormalities: bilateral mydriasis (58%), hyperexcitability or hyperesthesia (53%), ataxia (42%), tremors (42%), reduced mental awareness (32%) and seizures (16%) [1].
- Tachycardia occurred in 53%, hypertension in 21% and hyperthermia in 26%. Sinus tachycardia was the only arrhythmia reported. Hyperglycemia (21%) and raised lactate (47%) were found [1].

## Management

- 16 of 19 dogs were hospitalized for supportive care: isotonic fluids and sedatives or anxiolytics (diazepam, midazolam, acepromazine, chlorpromazine). Two needed further anticonvulsants and one an esmolol infusion [1].
- All 19 survived to discharge, with a median stay of 15 hours. The authors recommend hospitalization for monitoring because of the potential for seizures, hypertensive crisis and tachyarrhythmia [1].
- The series is limited to dogs with a positive urine cocaine test and comes from one hospital [1].
