# Respiratory mechanics and oxygen transport

## Why this matters

Oxygen readings, oxygen targets and chest findings do not transfer cleanly from human medicine to dogs: the dog's hemoglobin releases oxygen at a different pressure, and the textbook assumption about a dog's two pleural cavities is contested.

## Claims that differ from human practice

- Dog hemoglobin has a higher P50 than human hemoglobin (28.8 vs 26.6 mmHg in one study) and varies widely by breed (25.8 to 35.8), so a pulse-oximeter reading corresponds to a different arterial oxygen pressure than a human table suggests.
- Whether a dog's pneumothorax stays on one side is disputed: a 39-dog experiment found disease stayed confined unless the mediastinum was injured, yet 5 of 6 dogs in a 2019 ultrasound series had pneumothorax on both sides.

## Dog vs human compare rows

- **Oxygen release from hemoglobin (P50)** (human basis: cited): human: Human P50 at pH 7.4, PCO2 40 mmHg and 37 C was 26.6 mmHg in the same study. | canine: Dog P50 was 28.8 mmHg on average, ranging by breed from 25.8 (spaniel) to 35.8 (hound). Red-cell 2,3-DPG was higher in the dog (19.4 vs 13.5 umol/g Hb) and the oxygen exchange fraction between PO2 80 and 35 mmHg was 6.6 vs 5.5 vol%.
- **Pleural cavities and pneumothorax** (human basis: general-medical-knowledge): human: The human mediastinum is a barrier, so a one-sided pneumothorax usually stays one-sided. | canine: Evidence conflicts. In 39 dogs, hemothorax, pyothorax or pneumothorax stayed in one pleural cavity unless the mediastinum was mechanically injured, contrary to earlier reports of communication. In a 2019 case series of 6 dogs with pneumothorax, 5 had it bilaterally (mixed traumatic and spontaneous causes).
- **Oxygen supplementation target** (human basis: general-medical-knowledge): human: Adult oxygen targets are usually set in the mid-90s percent, with lower targets for some chronic lung disease. | canine: For dogs and cats with difficulty breathing or hypoxemia, the 2026 RECOVER first-aid guideline recommends oxygen during transport and, where monitoring exists, titrating to an SpO2 of 95 to 97% (strong recommendation, moderate quality evidence).
