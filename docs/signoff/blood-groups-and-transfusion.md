# Blood groups and transfusion

## Why this matters

Dogs do not have the ABO hazard that makes human transfusion unforgiving, but a mismatched first transfusion can quietly set up a severe reaction at the next one.

## Claims that differ from human practice

- Dogs lack naturally occurring red cell antibodies to DEA 1, so a first transfusion carries a negligible risk of an acute hemolytic reaction.
- Dogs can make antibodies within days of a transfusion, so any dog that has been transfused before needs a crossmatch.
- DEA 1 is the key group to type; blood typing before transfusion is recommended.

## Dog vs human compare rows

- **Natural antibodies** (human basis: general-medical-knowledge): human: ABO groups carry naturally occurring antibodies, so the first transfusion must be ABO-compatible. | canine: Dogs do not have naturally occurring DEA antibodies; the risk of an acute hemolytic transfusion reaction at the first transfusion is negligible. A pre-transfusion crossmatch study found no naturally occurring alloantibodies. One breed study (Italian Corso dogs) found suspected naturally occurring anti-DEA 7 antibodies in 32%.
- **Sensitization after a first mismatched transfusion** (human basis: general-medical-knowledge): human: Not applicable in the same way; ABO-incompatible blood is avoided at the first exposure. | canine: A mismatched first transfusion can sensitize the dog. Calculated sensitization probability was 24.9% in one Angolan population, and about 20 to 25% for most Italian breeds; the chance of an acute hemolytic reaction after a second incompatible transfusion was 6.21% in the Angolan population.
- **Speed of antibody formation** (human basis: general-medical-knowledge): human: Antibody responses to transfused red cells develop over days to weeks. | canine: Antibodies to red cells were detected as early as 1 day after transfusion. In a serial study, 12 of 21 dogs had a positive crossmatch within 4 days, and 11 of 25 DEA 1-matched recipients later became incompatible against other red cell antigens.
- **Which group to type** (human basis: general-medical-knowledge): human: ABO and Rh (D). | canine: DEA 1 is the most immunogenic and clinically important canine blood group. About 60% of dogs were DEA 1-positive in North America (59.6%) and Italy (61.2%). Weakly DEA 1-positive blood was immunogenic in a DEA 1-negative recipient.
