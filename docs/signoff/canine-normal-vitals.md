## Why this matters

Human vital-sign ranges do not transfer to dogs, and a resting respiratory rate above the healthy range is an early sign of trouble; only one healthy-dog respiratory rate dataset has been retrieved so far [1].

## Claims that differ from human practice

- In 114 apparently healthy adult dogs measured at home, the mean sleeping respiratory rate was 13 breaths/min and no dog averaged above 23; mean resting rate was 19 breaths/min [1].
- Instantaneous sleeping rates above 30 breaths/min were rare (3 dogs), so a sustained rate above 30 in a sleeping or calm dog is unusual [1].

## Recognition

- Respiratory rate: sleeping mean 13 (all dogs under 23); resting mean 19 breaths/min. Age, body weight and location did not change the sleeping rate [1].
- The study measured dogs at home; hospital or field values, and dogs that are panting, excited or hot, are not covered [1].
