# Exertional rhabdomyolysis

> DRAFT for review. Nothing here is approved. Do not use for patient care.

## Why this matters

Exertional muscle breakdown occurs in working dogs and is a listed complication of canine heatstroke, but the canine evidence is thin [1][2][6].

## Claims that differ from human practice

- Canine exertional rhabdomyolysis has been studied with creatine kinase and myoglobin in endurance sled dogs, which correlated strongly [1].
- Human hydration and outpatient rules are built on human cohorts and are not established for dogs [3][4][5].

## Recognition

- In endurance sled dogs, myoglobin and creatine kinase were higher in dogs with clinical rhabdomyolysis than in normal dogs and were strongly correlated (R = 0.91). Rhabdomyolysis appeared to occur early in endurance activity [1].
- Rhabdomyolysis is a listed complication of canine heatstroke, along with acute kidney injury [6].

## Management

- Cell-free DNA was not a useful marker in sled dogs, unlike myoglobin and creatine kinase [1].
- In trained search and rescue dogs, CK and AST did not change after 20 minutes of submaximal search work, consistent with conditioning protecting against enzyme changes [2].
- Human data, context only: acute kidney injury is a main complication, hydration is the commonest treatment, and outpatient care was safe in one cohort when admission creatinine was normal [3][4][5].
