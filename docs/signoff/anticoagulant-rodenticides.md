# Anticoagulant rodenticides

## Why this matters

Anticoagulant rodenticide poisoning is among the most common toxicoses in dogs and cats, and it is treatable when recognized [3].

## Claims that differ from human practice

- Coagulation times can normalise within hours with IV vitamin K1, without plasma, in the retrospective series retrieved [1][3].
- Response and treatment length vary with the dog and the agent, so treatment is individualized and coagulation is monitored [4].

## Recognition

- Long-acting anticoagulant rodenticides inhibit vitamin K epoxide reductase, and related bleeding can be a diagnostic challenge (human review) [5].
- Plasma toxin concentration at presentation varied widely in a dog series, and some dogs with characteristic coagulation disturbances and a positive response to vitamin K1 had no detectable toxin [4].

## Management

- In 58 dogs and cats given IV lecithin-solubilised vitamin K1, no adverse effects were recorded and all had coagulation times within range by 24 hours. Survival to discharge in dogs was 96% without and 85% with exogenous coagulation factors, a difference that was not significant [1].
- In 4 dogs, IV vitamin K1 normalised prothrombin time within an hour, but 2 had suspected anaphylactoid reactions and treatment stopped in one. Monitor for reactions [2].
- IV mixed-micelle phytomenadione had no recorded adverse reactions in its group of dogs and cats, comparable survival and a shorter hospital stay than traditional therapy with blood products [3].
- In a dog series, the vitamin K1 dose was reduced stepwise while prothrombin time stayed normal, and the authors concluded an individual approach is warranted [4].
