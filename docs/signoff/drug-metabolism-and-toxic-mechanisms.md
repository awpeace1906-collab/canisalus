## Why this matters

Drugs and foods that are safe or familiar in people can poison dogs through different mechanisms, so human dose tables and human safety margins do not transfer.

## Claims that differ from human practice

- Species differences in drug metabolism can make a drug with a wide margin in one species fatal in another at a fraction of the dose.
- Xylitol is safe in people but causes insulin-driven hypoglycemia and can cause liver failure in dogs.
- Acetaminophen overdose in dogs mainly causes methemoglobinemia and hemolysis, not the liver injury seen in most species.

## Dog vs human compare rows

- **Safety margins and dosing** (human basis: general-medical-knowledge): human: Human dose tables and safety margins are built on human pharmacology. | canine: Species differ in drug absorption, distribution, metabolism and excretion, especially phase II metabolism. Drugs with a wide therapeutic margin in some species can be fatal in another at a fraction of the dose. Enzyme isoforms in CYP1A, 2C, 2D and 3A families differ appreciably between species, and aldehyde oxidase was not detected in beagle dog liver.
- **Xylitol** (human basis: cited): human: Wide margin of safety in people. | canine: In dogs, xylitol is believed to stimulate excessive insulin secretion, causing severe hypoglycemia, potentially followed by acute hepatic failure and coagulopathy. Hypokalemia, hyperphosphatemia and thrombocytopenia can occur. Hypoglycemia can be delayed for hours after ingesting chewing gum. It is not seen in cats, rabbits or rodents.
- **Acetaminophen** (human basis: cited): human: Overdose mainly causes hepatotoxicity through the metabolite NAPQI. | canine: In dogs and cats, acetaminophen overdose primarily causes methemoglobinemia and hemolysis. In vitro, para-aminophenol rather than NAPQI produced methemoglobin, and deficient arylamine N-acetyltransferase activity in dogs and cats is proposed to contribute.
- **Breed-linked drug sensitivity** (human basis: general-medical-knowledge): human: Not a routine consideration when choosing a drug. | canine: A nonsense mutation described in Collies and related breeds abolishes P-glycoprotein function at the blood-brain barrier and is associated with increased susceptibility to neurotoxic effects of several drugs. See the MDR1 module.
