# Gastric dilatation-volvulus

## Why this matters

Large, deep-chested working breeds are at risk. GDV is described as rapidly fatal, and initial treatment is treating shock, decompressing the stomach and prompt surgery [8].

## Claims that differ from human practice

- There is no human equivalent; suspicion alone is a transport trigger.
- Decompression (orogastric tube or percutaneous trocar) is a bridge, not a fix; the dog still needs surgery.

## Recognition

- Possible signs: abdominal distension, non-productive retching, restlessness, signs of shock, tachypnea and dyspnea [8].
- Large and giant breeds are predisposed. In a UK emergency-care study the highest odds were Great Dane, Akita and Dogue de Bordeaux [6][8].

## Management

- Initial treatment includes treating shock and decompressing the stomach, with prompt surgery [8].
- Orogastric tubing and trocarization both had high success and low complication rates in a hospital series; either was described as acceptable [1]. This is hospital data, not field data.
- A trocar can be malpositioned and ineffective [2].
- Decompression is a bridge. Studies of delayed surgery examined when to operate, not whether, in hospitals with continuous monitoring [3][4].
