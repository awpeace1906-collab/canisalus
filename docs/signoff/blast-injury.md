## Why this matters

Explosions killed about a quarter of deployed military working dogs that died, and only military working dogs, not civilian or operational dogs in the comparison registry, sustained trauma from explosions [1][2].

## Claims that differ from human practice

- In deployed military working dogs, explosions accounted for 22.3% of injuries and were seen only in that group; explosion or blast caused 26.1% of deaths, and a larger share in Afghanistan (30.2%) than Iraq (17.2%) [1][2].

## Recognition

- Among 92 deployed military working dogs that died, explosion or blast was the second commonest external cause (26.1%) after gunshot wounds (31.5%) [1].
- Military working dogs and civilian dogs both had good prognosis for survival to discharge after trauma in a registry comparison [2].
