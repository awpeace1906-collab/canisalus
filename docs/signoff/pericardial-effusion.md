## Why this matters

Pericardial effusion in dogs can compress the heart (tamponade) and cause collapse; in a cardiology referral series about 42% of dogs with effusion had tamponade, and the cause was often neoplasia or severe valve disease [1].

## Claims that differ from human practice

- In dogs the commonest neoplasms behind effusions needing drainage are hemangiosarcoma and chemodectoma, plus idiopathic pericardial effusion; a collapsed older dog with abdominal distension or dyspnea can have a cardiac cause [2].
- Severe degenerative mitral and tricuspid valve disease was an equally common finding as neoplasia (35% vs 36% of 91 dogs) in one referral series [1].

## Recognition

- Common histories: lethargy, dyspnea, anorexia, collapse and abdominal distension [2].
- Echocardiography identified effusion in 91 of 5,304 referred dogs (1.7%); 71 were male. Tamponade was present in 38 of 91 (42%) [1].
- Tamponade means intrapericardial pressure equals or exceeds right ventricular diastolic filling pressure, lowering cardiac output [2].

## Management

- Pericardiocentesis stabilizes tamponade, relieves right-sided failure and yields fluid for diagnosis [2].
- Complications are rare but include cardiac puncture, arrhythmia, and laceration of a tumor or coronary artery causing intrapericardial hemorrhage or sudden death [2].
- In 28 echo-guided drainages with a 17G hemodialysis fistula needle, no adverse effects were seen over 48 hours [1].
