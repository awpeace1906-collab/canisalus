## Why this matters

Exertional hyperthermia is reported as one of the top three causes of death in working dogs [8], and mortality from heatstroke is around 50% despite early cooling and intensive care [7].

## Claims that differ from human practice

- Dogs cool by panting; in the studies retrieved, water immersion or wet skin with airflow cooled dogs better than alcohol on the paw pads.
- Cool first, transport second: a dog's temperature can keep rising after exercise stops.

## Recognition

- Heatstroke is core temperature above 41 °C with central nervous system dysfunction in one review [7].
- Common signs: acute collapse, tachypnea, spontaneous bleeding, shock signs, and mental changes from depression and disorientation to seizures, stupor and coma [7].
- Risk factors: hot and humid conditions, strenuous exercise, obesity, body weight over 15 kg, Labrador retrievers, brachycephalic breeds, upper airway obstruction and prolonged seizures [7].

## Management

- Cool first, transport second [4][6].
- Partial water immersion cooled working dogs faster than passive cooling or a cooling mat [3], and more efficiently than alcohol on the paw pads [1].
- Cold water (0.1 to 15 °C) cooled dogs effectively with no hypothermia or adverse effects in one canicross study [4].
- Voluntary head dunking with limited water ingestion cooled dogs with normal mental status who could pause panting. It is not an option for a dog that cannot [2].
- Tap water or alcohol on hairless skin, combined with a fan, cooled dogs from early on [5].
- Cool the vehicle before loading and keep air moving around the dog during transport [4].
