## Why this matters

A point-of-care blood result can look abnormal or normal by human ranges for the wrong reasons: puppies, sighthound breeds and the type of collection tube all change what is normal for a dog.

## Claims that differ from human practice

- Adult reference intervals should not be used for puppies: in 68 healthy puppies sodium, chloride and ionized magnesium were lower than adult values, potassium and ionized calcium higher, and glucose above adult values after day 4.
- Breed matters: Galgos Espanoles (Spanish greyhounds) had higher hematocrit, hemoglobin, red cell count and pH and lower platelets than other dogs, so a sighthound's numbers can look abnormal but be normal for the breed.
- Collection method matters: in 51 dogs, results from a heparin tube differed significantly from a blood gas syringe for pH, PCO2, PO2, bicarbonate, lactate, anion gap and ionized calcium, but not for sodium, potassium, chloride or glucose.
