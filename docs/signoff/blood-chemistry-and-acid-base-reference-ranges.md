## Why this matters

A point-of-care blood result can look abnormal or normal by human ranges for the wrong reasons: puppies, sighthound breeds and the type of collection tube all change what is normal for a dog.

## Claims that differ from human practice

- Adult reference intervals should not be used for puppies: in 68 healthy puppies sodium, chloride and ionized magnesium were lower than adult values, potassium and ionized calcium higher, and glucose above adult values after day 4.
- Breed matters: Galgos Espanoles (Spanish greyhounds) had higher hematocrit, hemoglobin, red cell count and pH and lower platelets than other dogs, so a sighthound's numbers can look abnormal but be normal for the breed.
- Collection method matters: in 51 dogs, results from a heparin tube differed significantly from a blood gas syringe for pH, PCO2, PO2, bicarbonate, lactate, anion gap and ionized calcium, but not for sodium, potassium, chloride or glucose.

- **Age** (human basis: general-medical-knowledge): human: Human laboratory ranges are also age-dependent, and pediatric ranges differ from adult ranges. | canine: In 68 healthy puppies tested from 4 to 84 days old, pH and bicarbonate were closest to adult values. - **Breed (sighthounds)** (human basis: general-medical-knowledge): human: Human reference ranges do not depend on breed. | canine: In 30 Galgos Espanoles compared with 20 dogs of other breeds, 5 of 24 parameters differed: higher hematocrit, hemoglobin, erythrocyte count and pH, and lower platelet count. The authors note differences are less striking than in greyhounds.
- **Sample collection for acid-base** (human basis: general-medical-knowledge): human: Arterial or venous gas samples are usually collected anaerobically in a blood gas syringe. | canine: In 51 healthy dogs, immediate analysis of lithium heparin tube samples matched blood gas syringe samples for standard bicarbonate, base excess, sodium, potassium, chloride, glucose and hemoglobin, but pH, PCO2, PO2, actual bicarbonate, extracellular base excess, ionized calcium, anion gap and lactate differed significantly.
- **Kidney markers differ by breed and size** (human basis: general-medical-knowledge): human: Human creatinine reference ranges depend on age, sex and muscle mass but not on breed. | canine: Serum creatinine is higher in Greyhounds than in nonsighthound breeds, and urine creatinine adjusted for urine specific gravity was about 22% higher in 98 healthy nonracing Greyhounds; urine protein-to-creatinine ratio was not significantly different. In a preliminary study of 40 healthy small and miniature dogs, Maltese had significantly higher serum creatinine than Yorkshire Terriers and Chihuahuas, and the authors advise combining creatinine, cystatin C, symmetric dimethylarginine, urine concentration, urine protein and breed when judging kidney function.
- **Urine concentration** (human basis: general-medical-knowledge): human: Healthy human urine osmolality is usually quoted up to about 1,200 mOsm/kg. | canine: In 1,991 clinically healthy dogs the 95% reference interval for urine osmolality was 369 to 2,416 mOsm/kg in young and adult dogs and 366 to 2,178 in seniors. Senior dogs and neutered females had lower values, and urine specific gravity correlated with osmolality (r = 0.96) but agreed inconsistently.
