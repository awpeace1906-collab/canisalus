# Seizures and drug resistance

## Why this matters

Prolonged seizures become harder to stop the longer they last, partly because the canine brain pumps antiseizure drugs back out, so the first minutes and the route of the first drug matter.

## Claims that differ from human practice

- In dogs, status epilepticus is defined from 5 minutes (or 2 or more seizures without recovery), the same early treatment threshold as in people; permanent injury is expected later, from about 30 minutes.
- After status epilepticus, the drug-efflux pump P-glycoprotein rose by 87 to 166% in the canine brain, which limits antiseizure drug levels reaching the brain; giving a drug by a route that bypasses this barrier, such as intranasal, is one proposed way around it.

## Dog vs human compare rows

- **When a seizure becomes an emergency** (human basis: cited): human: The International League Against Epilepsy defines status epilepticus as a seizure lasting over 5 minutes, with long-term consequences such as neuronal death possible after 30 minutes. | canine: The 2023 ACVIM consensus uses the same 5-minute time frame, and the veterinary task force also counts 2 or more seizures without recovery of consciousness in between. The 5-minute cut-off is meant to start treatment before injury at 30 minutes and before drug resistance develops. Among dogs presenting to hospital for seizures, 16.5% manifest status epilepticus, and it was the first sign of epilepsy in 58% in one report; overall mortality of dogs with status epilepticus has been reported at 25.3 to 38.5%.
- **Drug efflux at the blood-brain barrier** (human basis: general-medical-knowledge): human: Prolonged seizures in people are also associated with loss of GABA inhibition, receptor changes and over-expression of blood-brain barrier drug transporters that contribute to drug resistance. | canine: A rise in P-glycoprotein of 87 to 166% has been demonstrated in the canine brain after status epilepticus, increasing efflux of antiseizure drugs and limiting their brain levels. Benzodiazepine effect also falls with time because receptor subunits are internalized, so drugs acting at other sites are needed in refractory stages.
- **Causes of status epilepticus in dogs** (human basis: general-medical-knowledge): human: Status epilepticus in people arises from known epilepsy, acute brain insults or other acute causes. | canine: Status epilepticus occurred in 27 to 59% of dogs with idiopathic epilepsy, 32 to 40% with structural epilepsy and 7 to 23% with reactive seizures (such as toxins or metabolic causes). About 0.5 to 2.6% of dogs admitted to emergency hospitals have it.
