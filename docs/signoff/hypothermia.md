## Why this matters

Hypothermia can follow environmental exposure, illness, medications, anesthesia or trauma, and even severely affected dogs, including those in cardiopulmonary arrest, can recover completely with appropriate therapy [1].

## Claims that differ from human practice

- Ordinary thermometers can miss hypothermia: nontraditional thermometers may be needed to diagnose it [1].
- Even dogs in cardiopulmonary arrest from hypothermia can recover fully, so resuscitation may need to be prolonged [1].

## Recognition

- Hypothermia is diagnosed from presenting temperature or clinical signs, and may need nontraditional thermometers [1].
- Human trauma data: hypothermia on admission was independently associated with higher mortality (23.4% vs 2.3%) in a 3-million-patient registry. This is human data and is context only [2].

## Management

- Rewarming is the primary treatment for accidental hypothermia, from passive surface rewarming to extracorporeal rewarming. The goal is to return core temperature to a level that restores normal physiologic function [1].
- Intravenous fluids and other supportive care are typically indicated. If cardiopulmonary arrest is present, prolonged resuscitation may be required [1].
- In secondary hypothermia, reversing the underlying cause is important [1].
- There are few prognostic indicators in hypothermic dogs, so early discouraging findings should not stop care [1].
