## What the sources support (candidate statements)

- **Recognition (G8).** Possible signs: abdominal distension, non-productive retching, restlessness, signs of shock, tachypnea, dyspnea. Described as rapidly fatal. Common in large and giant breeds.
- **Risk (G6).** Prevalence 0.64% among UK emergency-care dogs. Highest odds: Great Dane (OR 114.3), Akita (OR 84.4), Dogue de Bordeaux (OR 82.9). Odds rose with age up to 12 years. Reviewers: no working-breed data was retrieved. Add if a source exists.
- **Initial management (G8).** Treat shock and decompress the stomach; surgery promptly.
- **Both field-relevant decompression techniques work (G1).** In 116 dogs (orogastric tube n=31, trocar n=39, both n=46): tubing was successful in 59 dogs (75.5%) and unsuccessful in 18 (23.4%); trocarization was successful in 73 dogs (86%) and unsuccessful in 12 (14%). Figures as stated in the abstract. No gastric perforation was seen at surgery with either. One splenic laceration after trocarization, not requiring treatment. No esophageal rupture or aspiration pneumonia. No difference in survival to discharge. G1 concludes either is acceptable. *This is hospital data, not field data.*
- **Trocar complications exist (G2).** In 16 dogs, trocar decompression was successful in 7/8, with one malpositioned, ineffective trocar. Temporary gastropexy with a gastrostomy catheter had two inadvertent splenic or jejunal placements. All 16 survived at least 2 weeks.
- **Decompression is a bridge, not a cure.** G3 and G4 studied delayed surgery after decompression. G3: no survival difference between surgery at a median 2.1 h vs 9.8 h after presentation (70/89 vs 60/73 discharged). G4: mortality 9% (3/35) with staged surgery at a mean 22.3 h, but the authors say careful case selection matters and found no reliable preoperative selection criteria. **Neither supports skipping surgery.** They describe hospital settings with continuous monitoring.
- **Outcomes when treated (G5, G6).** G5: 64.1% survived overall, and most nonsurvival was euthanasia before surgery without intent to treat; 83.5% survived among dogs not euthanized preoperatively. G6: 79.3% of surgical cases survived to discharge.
- **Lactate is prognostic (G7).** Initial lactate <= 9.0 mmol/L: 90% survived; > 9.0: 54%. In the high group, post-resuscitation lactate and its change predicted survival. Not a field measurement for most users.

## What changes from human (candidates from the existing stub, now with evidence)

1. "No human equivalent; suspicion alone is a transport trigger": consistent with G8 (rapidly fatal) but no source explicitly recommends a "go now" rule. **Reviewer to confirm the recommendation and its source.**
2. "Decompression is a bridge, not a fix; the dog still needs surgery": supported by G8 and by G3/G4 being studies of when, not whether, to operate.

## Not retrieved

- ACVECC/VECCS or other consensus guidance on GDV.
- Any field or prehospital study of trocar or orogastric decompression by non-veterinarians.
- Trocar site, needle size, orogastric tube sizing and technique. **The framework's open question on trocar scope cannot be answered from this evidence alone.** G1 and G2 are hospital studies.
- Whether flight/CCT crews should decompress. No source retrieved.
- Fluid volumes and any drugs. None proposed.
