**Rank-1 source exists but was not read:** RECOVER First Aid Guidelines: Acute Allergy and Anaphylaxis in Dogs and Cats (Burkitt-Creedon 2026, JVECC). Abstract: 6 PICO questions, 12 treatment recommendations, 7 on low or very low quality evidence and 5 expert opinion, with an algorithm. **Full text needed for the drug, dose and route.**

**Canine vs human contrast (from Turner 2021, 232 dogs):** vasoconstrictive shock 49% vs vasodilatory 13%; gallbladder wall edema on ultrasound in 59% of dogs scanned; dermatological signs absent in about a quarter.

**Caution on outcomes:** Kadowaki 2026 (49 dogs, 91.8% survival) and Smith 2020 (67 dogs, 85%) are retrospective and cannot show whether epinephrine timing changes survival. Fosset 2023 found no association of glucocorticoids with recovery.

**Not retrieved:** dose and route of epinephrine; observation period; biphasic reactions.

## 2026 RECOVER first-aid anaphylaxis guideline: full text read (added 2026-09-30)

Source: Burkitt-Creedon 2026, JVECC 36 Suppl 1:S63-S89 (PMID 42640815, open access in PMC13505870). Read via the PubMed full-text tool; recommendation blocks and the algorithm discussion were read, the reference list and some justification sections were skimmed.

- 12 recommendations across 6 PICO questions; 7 low or very low quality, 5 expert opinion. Graded strengths are copied into the module.
- The guideline does **not** give a stated epinephrine dose as a treatment recommendation.
- Doses that appear in the text, for the vet reviewer only (not in the module): 0.01 mg/kg epinephrine is the bolus dose used in the cited canine experimental shock models (also 0.025 mg/kg in one crossover study); it also appears in the arithmetic for pediatric 0.15 mg autoinjectors in a 15 kg animal. Low-dose glucocorticoid for uncomplicated reactions that fail H1 blockade is stated as up to 0.5 mg/kg/day prednisolone equivalent for up to 5 days.
