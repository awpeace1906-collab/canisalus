# Chocolate and methylxanthines (tox-common / reversible)

Module: `content/modules/tox-common/chocolate.json`. Draft. Not reviewed.

## Evidence retrieved (abstracts)

Weingart 2021 (156 dogs, *J Small Anim Pract*); Bates 2023 (*Companion Animal* review); Genareo 2025 (case: severe hypernatremia after activated charcoal); Noble 2017 (*Vet Rec*, seasonal exposure). Also a 2008 Iranian case series and 2001 review that give thresholds, plus Indonesian-language reviews (not used).

## Reported thresholds (not in the module; reviewer decision)

A 2008 four-dog case series abstract (Ghazaleh, *Iran J Vet Med*) states, citing general toxicology references: mild signs may be seen at about 20 mg/kg of methylxanthines, cardiotoxic effects at 40 to 50 mg/kg, seizures at 60 mg/kg or more, and the LD50 of caffeine and theobromine is reported as 100 to 200 mg/kg with severe signs possible at much lower doses. The Genareo 2025 dog had ingested about 88 mg/kg theobromine and developed tremors and seizures. Methylxanthine content by chocolate type is given per gram in the same 2008 abstract. **Second-hand, low-quality source. Reviewers to supply an authoritative table or decide none belongs in the app.**

## What the sources support

See the module. The 156-dog series: most dogs had no signs; symptomatic dogs mostly ate dark or bitter chocolate; 43 of 44 symptomatic dogs survived after decontamination and symptomatic care.

## Not retrieved

Any guideline; time window for effective decontamination; esmolol and other drug specifics; pets with chocolate-covered raisins or coffee beans (mentioned as an additional hazard by Bates).
