## What the abstracts support

- **Scope.** The 2024 guidelines update the 2012 RECOVER guidelines using GRADE; 40 worksheets across BLS, ALS and monitoring produced 90 treatment recommendations (C1). BLS: 20 questions, 32 recommendations, 25 on compressions and 7 on ventilation (C2).
- **Evidence is weak.** BLS and ALS recommendations rest predominantly on very low-quality evidence and expert opinion (C2, C3).
- **Compressions (C2).** Emphasis on high-quality, uninterrupted compressions. A modification to compression technique is suggested for wide-chested dogs; recommendations are conformation-specific. *The specific technique is in the full text, not the abstract.*
- **Ventilation (C1, C2).** Ventilate all animals. When intubation is not possible, bag-mask ventilation with a tight-fitting facemask and oxygen is recommended over mouth-to-nose ventilation.
- **Do not wait for a pulse (C4).** Monitoring authors continue to support starting compressions without pulse palpation. End-tidal CO2 is useful to verify intubation, identify ROSC, evaluate CPR quality and guide BLS.
- **Drugs (C1, C3).** High-dose epinephrine is no longer recommended, even after prolonged CPR; atropine, if used, only once. For refractory shockable rhythm: double the defibrillator dose once, then consider vasopressin (or epinephrine), esmolol, lidocaine in dogs. **An updated drug dosing table exists in the guideline (C1). No dose is reproduced here.**
- **Post-arrest (C5).** Good functional outcomes are possible, including after out-of-hospital arrest and prolonged CPR. Stabilization and neurological improvement may take 48-72 h.

## What changes from human (candidates)

1. Compression technique depends on chest conformation (C2). *Framework claim "lateral recumbency for most dogs" is not in the abstracts; the reviewer must confirm from the full text.*
2. Start compressions without a pulse check (C4).
3. Bag-mask ventilation, not mouth-to-nose, when not intubated (C2).
